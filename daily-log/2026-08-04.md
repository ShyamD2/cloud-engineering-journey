---

## The problem I ran into & how I solved it
* **What I was trying to do:** Resolving an internal domain name on my local VM
* **The error:** ``curl: (6) Could not resolve host: internal.lab.local`.`
* **What actually caused it:** `systemd-resolved` was forwarding all queries to a public upstream DNS that had no record of my local private zone.
* **How I fixed it:** Added routing domain entry `Domains=~lab.local` in `/etc/systemd/resolved.conf` pointing specifically to my internal DNS IP (`10.0.0.2`), then restarted `systemd-resolved`.
* **Takeaway / lesson learned:** The `~domain` syntax in `systemd-resolved` routes specific domain queries to specific internal DNS servers without leaking private hostnames to public DNS.

---

## Key takeaways from today
- DNS lookups use UDP port 53 by default, but fall back to TCP port 53 if the response exceeds 512 bytes.
- Negative caching (SOA minimum TTL) means if a domain failed to resolve, the failure itself gets cached for a bit.
