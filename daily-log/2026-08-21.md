# 2026-08-21 — Day 21: Auto Scaling Groups & Target Tracking Policies

Hooked up an Auto Scaling Group (ASG) to my ALB target group today. Scalability isn't just about handling spikes; it's also about replacing unhealthy instances automatically.

---

## What I worked on today
- Created an ASG with `min=2`, `desired=2`, `max=6` spanning two AZs.
- Configured a Target Tracking scaling policy targeting 50% average CPU utilization.
- Simulated a CPU spike using `stress-ng` on an instance to watch the ASG launch replacement capacity.

---

## Commands I ran
```bash
aws autoscaling create-auto-scaling-group --auto-scaling-group-name api-asg --launch-template LaunchTemplateName=prod-web-template,Version=1 --min-size 2 --max-size 6 --desired-capacity 2 --target-group-arns arn:aws:... --vpc-zone-identifier 'subnet-priv-1,subnet-priv-2'
sudo stress-ng --cpu 4 --timeout 300s
```

---

## The problem I ran into & how I solved it
* **What I was trying to do:** Testing scale-out under stress
* **The error:** `CloudWatch showed high CPU, but the ASG failed to launch new instances.`
* **What actually caused it:** The instance role in the Launch Template had a permission typo when pulling credentials.
* **How I fixed it:** Checked ASG Activity History with `aws autoscaling describe-scaling-activities`, fixed the IAM policy, and re-tested.
* **Takeaway / lesson learned:** `aws autoscaling describe-scaling-activities` is the first place to look whenever an ASG fails to launch or terminate instances.

---

## Key takeaways from today
- Target Tracking policies behave like a thermostat: AWS handles the math to keep metrics at your desired target.
- Cooldown periods prevent scaling thrash (adding and removing instances too quickly).
