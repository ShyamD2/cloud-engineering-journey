## The problem I ran into & how I solved it
* **What I was trying to do:** Ensure documentation across multiple repositories (Portfolio, Cloud Journey, DriftWarden, and JARVIS) stays in perfect sync without drifting.
* **The issue:** As new projects like DriftWarden and JARVIS reports were released, old stubs in the engineering journal lagged behind and didn't reflect the latest benchmarks and architecture.
* **How I fixed it:** Treated documentation with the same GitOps discipline as Infrastructure as Code: established a single source of truth for project metrics (such as the 90.35 ns/op KubeForecast latency and the 183 passing JARVIS tests) and linked all repositories directly to the centralized PDF reports and live demos.
* **Takeaway / lesson learned:** Documentation debt is technical debt. A cloud engineer's proof of work is only as credible as the accuracy and currency of their public repositories.
