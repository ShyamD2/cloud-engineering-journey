## The problem I ran into & how I solved it
* **What I was trying to do:** Have J.A.R.V.I.S. comfortably read detailed technical summaries and multi-step plans without cutting itself off or ignoring operator voice commands to stop speaking.
* **The error:** Long answers would abruptly cut off after 7.5 seconds and restart from the first sentence, and saying *"Stop"* or *"Quiet"* was completely ignored by the system while audio was playing through the speakers.
* **What actually caused it:**
  1. `services/jarvis_core/routes/query.py` had an uncalibrated 7.5-second client timeout designed for single-sentence queries.
  2. Monolithic TTS synthesis generated one massive audio buffer, locking the audio player (`pygame.mixer`) until completion.
  3. `wake_word_daemon.py` strictly checked for the `"hey jarvis"` activation token before listening to any words, discarding immediate interrupt keywords.
* **How I resolved / planned the solution:**
  1. Documented the failure modes in detail in `reports/DAY_13_REPORT_2026-09-21.md`.
  2. Formulated the architectural blueprint for a centralized, sub-10ms `InterruptService` capable of clause-level progressive streaming and wake-word-free keyword acoustic interruption.
  3. Stabilized underlying mock and contract tests across all 35 phases to ensure zero regressions before implementing the interrupt engine.
* **Takeaway / lesson learned:** In voice conversational systems, output recitations and acoustic input listeners must never operate as decoupled silos. The listener must know the exact state of the speaker, and long-form audio must be streamed clause-by-clause to preserve instant operator agency.

---

## Key takeaways from today
- **Monolithic generation kills responsiveness**: Never synthesize an entire 5-sentence response into a single audio buffer. Splitting into natural clauses (punctuation-delimited) enables low initial latency (<400ms to first sound) and graceful cancellation.
- **Barge-in requires contextual state awareness**: When an assistant is actively talking, the wake-word requirement should be dropped for negative/stop acoustic keywords like *"stop"*, *"quiet"*, and *"pause"*.
